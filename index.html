How a lesson is made

  1. You send one question, typed or as a photo of a textbook or notebook page.
  2. The question is read aloud and marked on the board, word by word.
  3. The diagram is drawn while it is explained, not shown finished.
  4. The rule or method is named before it is used, and every line is spoken as it is written.
  5. The answer is boxed, and an independent second solver checks it before the lesson is kept.

What it covers

Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics and Biology · NCERT classes 6–12 · JEE and NEET · Hinglish, Hindi, English and six more Indian languages.

Your question stays yours. A question you send is never published without your permission, including any photograph of your own notebook. What we hold, and for how long, is set out in the Privacy Policy.

Our use of the YouTube API

SnapSolve publishes its own lessons to its own channel using YouTube API Services. It reads no other channel's data. See the Privacy Policy and Terms of Service, and Google's own Privacy Policy.
