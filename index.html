Privacy Policy

Last updated 27 September 2026

SnapSolve turns one school or exam question into a short whiteboard video that explains it. This page says exactly what we hold when you send a doubt, for how long, and what we never do with it.

Who runs this

SnapSolve is run by an individual developer, not a company. Contact: sripadkulkarni9124@gmail.com.

What we collect

WhatWhy
The question you send — typed text, or a photo of a textbook or notebook page It is the input. Without it there is no lesson.
The subject, class, chapter and language worked out from that question To teach it at the right level and in the right language.
The lesson produced: the script, the narration audio, the video So the lesson can be replayed and improved.
A per-request record: a timestamp, an anonymous identifier, whether the lesson succeeded or failed, how long it took and what it cost To find faults and to stop abuse.

The anonymous identifier. If you are not signed in, requests are grouped under a short hash derived from your IP address and browser user-agent. The IP address itself is not stored — only the hash, which cannot be reversed back to it. It exists so we can see that one person sent five doubts, not who that person is.

What we never collect

  • No name, phone number, school, or date of birth.
  • No payment details. SnapSolve is free.
  • No advertising or tracking cookies, and no third-party analytics.
  • No other YouTube user's data. Our YouTube access is limited to our own channel (see below).

Who else sees it

The question and its photo are sent to Google's Gemini API, which reads the question and plans the explanation. Google processes it under its API terms. Nothing is sent to any other third party, and we do not sell or share data with anyone.

Our use of the YouTube API

SnapSolve publishes its own lessons to its own YouTube channel using YouTube API Services. It uploads videos, edits their titles, descriptions and tags, and arranges them into playlists — all on the one channel we own. It does not read, store, or display any other channel's data, and it never acts on behalf of a viewer.

By using a service built on the YouTube API you are also bound by the YouTube Terms of Service. Google's own handling of data is described in the Google Privacy Policy. You can review and revoke what third-party applications may do with your Google account at Google security settings.

Published lessons

Some lessons are published publicly on our YouTube channel. A lesson is only published when its question is a general academic one. A question sent by a student is never published without their permission — including any photograph they submitted. If a lesson of yours is public and you want it taken down, email us and it will be removed.

How long it is kept

  • Questions, photos and lessons: kept while the service needs them to show you your lesson, and deleted on request.
  • Request records: kept up to 12 months, then deleted.
  • Published videos: kept until removed by us or on request.

Your choices

Email sripadkulkarni9124@gmail.com to ask what is held about you, to have it deleted, or to have a published lesson taken down. We answer within 30 days.

Children

SnapSolve is used by school students. We collect no name, contact detail or identifier that would identify a child, and we show no advertising. If you believe a child's personal information has reached us, email us and it will be deleted.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it.
